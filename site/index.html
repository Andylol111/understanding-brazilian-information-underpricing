Election-night counts and market prices

While a count runs, a model projects each race's final result from the places already counted. These pages test it on every night: how close the projection was, how often its 90% band held, how its probabilities compared with Polymarket's, and what its trading signals would have earned.

How much each result can prove depends on when the model's settings were fixed. Some were chosen on the same night's data, some were frozen before that data was examined but after the outcome was known, and some were frozen before the election: only the last kind can show that the markets can be beaten. Each dashboard says which applies under Method & sources; the rules are in prereg/NOTES.md.

What is measured

For every reporting unit (a US county, a Brazilian municipality, an Israeli locality) the model combines the votes counted so far with that unit's result in a comparable earlier election to project its final result, then adds the units up. The projection carries a 90% band, and the band gives a probability that each candidate wins.

The raw count is the running total on the official results page. Early in the night it can point the wrong way, because places do not report in random order.

The market is the Polymarket price for the same outcome, recorded every minute and read as a probability. Where Kalshi lists the same race, its price is shown too, and the two venues are compared with each other. Kalshi data: Kalshi Inc.

Replays use only what was public at each minute: count updates by the time they were published, prices by the time they were quoted. Only official or openly licensed data is published here.

The same statistics for every election

Calibration
Share of minutes between 1% and 95% counted in which the final result fell inside the model's 90% band, and the mean error in standard deviations.
Settle time
The first minute from which the model and each contested market stay at 0.9 or more on the eventual winner, counted from 1% of the vote. The difference is the model's lead. A market is “decided before the count” instead if the eventual outcome was at least 0.90 at the first minute at or after official poll close and stayed at least 0.80 through the count; those markets are excluded from lead comparisons.
Catch-up
When model and market disagree, the share of the gap the price closes over the next 30 minutes.
Early error
How far the projection and the raw count were from the final share at 1%, 5%, 10% and 25% counted.
Capacity and profit
Dollars that actually traded at each signal's price within 30 minutes, and what fixed stakes would have returned after costs.
