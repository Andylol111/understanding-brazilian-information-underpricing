Election nights

Election-night counts and market prices

Election results are published unit by unit while the count runs. A model projects each race's final result from the units already in; the dashboards below compare that projection, minute by minute, with Polymarket prices, to measure how long the prices take to reflect what the count already shows.

Code, tests, frozen model settings and pre-registrations: GitHub.

What is measured

For every reporting unit (a US county, a Brazilian municipality, an Israeli locality) the model combines the votes counted so far with that unit's result in a comparable earlier election to project its final result, then adds the units up. The projection carries a 90% band, and the band gives a probability that each candidate wins.

The raw count is the running total on the official results page. Early in the night it can point the wrong way, because places do not report in random order.

The market is the Polymarket price for the same outcome, recorded every minute and read as a probability. Kalshi lists many of the same races, but its data terms do not allow republishing its prices, so these pages show none; statistics that would need them (Kalshi catch-up, Polymarket–Kalshi arbitrage) are marked as not computed, with that reason.

The same statistics for every election

Calibration
Share of minutes between 1% and 95% counted in which the final result fell inside the model's 90% band (about 90% if the band has the right width), and the mean error in standard deviations (about 0 if the model is not biased one way).
Settle time
The first minute from which the model and each market stay at 0.9 or more on the eventual winner, and from which the raw count's leader no longer changes. The difference between model and market is the model's lead.
Catch-up
When model and market disagree, the share of the gap the market price closes over the next 30 minutes, with a 95% interval.
Early error
How far the model's projection and the raw count were from the final share when 1%, 5%, 10% and 25% had been counted, in percentage points.
Capacity
Dollars that actually traded at the price of each model signal, and at 2¢ and 5¢ worse, in the 30 minutes after it.
Arbitrage
Minutes in which buying one side on Polymarket and the other side on Kalshi cost less than $1 after both platforms' fees, and how much could have been filled.

Rules applied to every election

  • Model settings for a night are written down and frozen (their file hashes go into a public registry) before that night's count is examined.
  • Every result carries a label: in-sample settings were chosen on this data; pre-specified settings were frozen before the data was examined, but the outcome was already known; forward settings were frozen before the outcome existed; not pre-registered settings were chosen on earlier elections and scored once, but were not frozen in the repository before scoring; model only no market prices to compare with; market only no count model was run, because there is no official count over time to run it on; the page shows market prices over the night and the official final results. Only forward results can support a claim that the markets can be beaten.
  • Replays use only what was public at each minute: count updates by the time they were published, prices by the time they were quoted.
  • Only official or openly licensed data is published here. Each dashboard lists its sources and licences.
