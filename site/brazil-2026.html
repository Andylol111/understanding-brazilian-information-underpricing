Election nights / Brazil 2026 article

How quickly Polymarket priced Brazil's 2026 vote count

I replayed both of Brazil's 2026 election nights minute by minute from the official polling-section results and compared a count-based model with Polymarket prices.

Code, tests and pre-registrations: GitHub.

Summary

  • When the model and the price disagreed, the price had moved 40% of the way towards the model 30 minutes later on runoff night (12 markets, settings frozen beforehand) and 55% on the first round (8 markets, the night the settings were chosen on).
  • On the runoff, from the point where 1% of sections were counted (19:04), the model was 90% sure of Lula's win about 80 minutes before the market was.
  • The underpricing could be traded. After the model's signal, real buyers bought $3.1 million of Lula "yes" on runoff night at prices at least 5¢ below the model's probability, and made $892,000 on it. $10,000 that took a tenth of each of those trades would have ended at $20,762. Almost all of it was one market, the national "Lula wins", on one outcome.

Runoff night

Brazil counts electronic ballots quickly and publishes results by polling section. Sections do not arrive in random order: early on, the running national tally is dominated by the states that report first, so it can point the wrong way for an hour or more.

The model corrects for that. It projects each municipality's final result from the sections already in, using only information available before the night (for the runoff, each municipality's first-round result) to fill in what hasn't reported. The raw tally is what the official results page showed. The market is the Polymarket price.

At 19:04 on runoff night, with 1.5% of sections counted, the model put Lula's chance of winning at 0.95; the market said 0.38. The market crossed 0.5 at 19:10. Then from 19:45 to 20:23, between 14% and 46% counted, the raw tally showed Flávio ahead, and the market fell from 0.86 to 0.62 while the model's projection barely moved. The market settled above 0.9 at 20:24, the same minute the raw tally turned back to Lula. He won 51.79–48.21.

The market's moves in this period follow the raw tally rather than the projection.

Checks against price noise

The gap and the move both contain the current price, so a stale or bouncing price alone would produce a positive slope even in a market that had priced the count. Each check below removes that shared term in a different way. If noise explained the result, the slope would vanish when the move is measured from a minute later, and the placebos would show a similar slope. Neither happens.

All replayed races

Ten races from three elections. For 2026 the comparison is with Polymarket. For 2022 no Polymarket prices survive, so the comparison is with the raw tally. Each 2026 runoff replay ran once, with settings frozen before its data was downloaded.

How much could have been traded

For every real Polymarket trade after 1% of sections were counted, I asked whether the buyer paid at least 5¢ less than the model's probability for that side, using the model's figure from at least 60 seconds before the trade, fees included. Those buyers were buying underpriced shares; this is what they bought and what it paid:

Nightunderpriced volume boughtprofit on it$10,000, a tenth of each trade
Runoff, presidential and state markets$3,107,751+$892,007$20,762
First round (settings chosen on this night)$143,228+$52,005$14,763
Governor runoffs$695+$319$10,032

On the runoff the edge lasted: between 19:00 and 20:45 the model gave Lula 0.90–0.99 while the market traded at 0.69–0.76. With a stricter test (at least 10¢ below the model, a 5-minute delay, a tenth of each trade) $10,000 still ends at $16,554. The volume is a ceiling for one trader, since other buyers took it, and 99% of it was the national market, so the result rests on one outcome. This buying rule was defined after the results were known. An earlier, stricter test that only accepted the exact minute price at the signal found almost nothing ($0 on the runoff), because by the signal the market already traded 2–4¢ above that price, still far below the model.

Other Polymarket categories

To see whether the Brazil result reflects a general pattern, I registered three tests across ten Polymarket categories before looking at held-out data. Each test's hypothesis, sample and statistic were hashed in advance and run once:

  • Thin markets over-react to sharp moves and then revert: significant in 6 of 10 categories after multiple-testing correction, same sign in all ten. pre-registered
  • "Mentions" markets under-price near-certain outcomes: +2.1% per dollar after spread and fees, but only $8.3k fillable across 1,851 entries. pre-registered
  • Deep markets reacting too slowly in general: not supported. pre-registered

The slow pricing on Brazil's count nights is therefore a specific case; this sample does not find it across deep markets in general.

Evidence labels

  • in-sample The model's settings were chosen on the first-round night. It shows the method can work, not that it will.
  • pre-specified Settings frozen before the data was examined, each run once. Outcomes were already known when the replays ran.
  • pre-registered Tests fixed and hashed before seeing held-out data; the hashes and every look are in the repository.
