# Poll start: final polls as the starting estimate (sensitivity v3)

Generated by `analysis/poll_prior_report.py` from the published site data, the poll tables in `data_public/` and the `configs/<id>_v3.toml` files. Written after every one of these results was known: **a sensitivity, not a forecast**. v1 stays the headline in every replay; the dashed `v3` line and its own 90% mark sit next to it (Show bar, scrubber).

## What it is

The same model as v1 (same prior, `tau`, `rho`, `kappa`, same count states), started from the final polls instead of the previous election alone (the idea of Germany v3, `docs/germany.md`, without re-fitting anything). The rule was written in `analysis/poll_priors.py` before any of these nights was replayed with it:

1. **Poll average.** Every poll of the country's table (Wikipedia, CC BY-SA, one row per poll with pollster, fieldwork dates and sample as printed, `data_public/<cc>_polls_<year>.csv`) whose fieldwork ended in the 14 days before election day, so it was published before the vote. For each poll the two-way share of side A, A / (A + B), uses the columns printed (undecided and others drop out); the average is the plain mean, no weighting.
2. **Poll error.** The same average for every earlier election of that country with a usable poll table, against the official result's two-way share (`data_public/<cc>_poll_results.csv`). The sd of the starting estimate is the root-mean-square of those errors, bias not corrected, no floor.
3. **Start.** `brunder.nowcast.Params.swing_prior_mean / swing_prior_sd` (the US poll model's mechanism): mean = poll two-way share minus the prior's vote-weighted two-way share, sd from step 2. The counted votes' own swing takes over as they arrive; `count_scaled_terms` lets the reporting-order error `tau` enter in proportion to the weight the counted votes have in the swing, so **before any count the uncertainty is the poll error alone** and the chance exists from poll close. Brazilian state races use the national swing prior with the full state-deviation terms (a state's own deviation is not a poll error). Hungary's district and list models share one national error, so seats and list move together.

No count, price or exit poll of the night is an input. The settings of v1 are not re-fitted, so this isolates what the polls add.

## Data and caveats

- Poll tables come from English Wikipedia (some earlier ones from the Spanish page or the election article, named in each CSV header together with the revision id and the table choice); the polls are the pollsters' own. Dates and samples are as printed; where a page prints none the CSV says so.
- Mexico 2012 prints only the month, so its three releases flagged as the final pre-election polls are used; Mexico 2006 prints the publication date (no fieldwork dates). Hungary 2014 and 2018 have two to four polls in the last 14 days; Hungarian polls are decided voters.
- Each earlier election's A / B pair is fixed in `analysis/poll_priors.py` (Mexico: the left candidate against all other main candidates, as the model's prior does; Hungary: Fidesz-KDNP against the runner-up list; Brazil and Poland: the two sides of the replay). Few elections per country: the sd is a rough guide, and an error of the same sign three times (Mexico) or a very large one (Brazil 2014, Hungary 2022) moves it a lot.
- State races in Brazil use the national poll swing; no state polls are used.
- It is retrospective and tuned to nothing: the rule was fixed before the replays, and v1, the headline, is never changed.
